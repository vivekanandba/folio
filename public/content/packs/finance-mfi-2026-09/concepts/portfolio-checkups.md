# The yearly checkup — four checks, and what weighting hides

> Feedback that arrives too fast produces action that is mostly noise.

> [!key] Only **four checks actually matter**, and they run on a **yearly clock**: how much you contribute each month, whether your **allocation has drifted**, whether holdings are **genuinely diversified underneath the surface**, and what **capital-gains tax is building up**. The daily number at the top of the screen "is not a check — it is anxiety with a green-and-red interface."

## What "underneath the surface" means: the weighting lesson

Two indices own **exactly the same 50 stocks** — and over the past year the **Nifty 50 lost 2.3%** while the **Equal Weight version gained 8.2%** (three years: 6.7% vs **14.0% a year**). Nothing differs but *how much of each*: the top 10 are **more than half** of the Nifty 50; the bottom 10 under 7%. In the Equal Weight index, every group of ten is roughly a fifth.

> [!warn] Every index carries **a judgement about what deserves weight** — market cap rewards whatever grew, whether fundamentals or fashion drove it. Owning "the index" is owning that judgement. Diversification is a property of *weights*, not of stock counts — the same lesson at portfolio level: your five funds may be one bet wearing five names.

> [!more] Volatility vs risk — the veteran's distinction
> The multi-decadal investor who asked his shocked RIA for **quarterly summaries only** distinguishes volatility (a feature of markets) from risk (a change in what your money can do). Monthly statements measure the former and provoke reactions to it. The four checks measure the latter.

## Investor move

- Review on a **yearly clock**: contribution, drift, true overlap, tax.
- Check diversification by **weights and overlap**, not fund count.
- Starve the daily number; feed the four checks.
