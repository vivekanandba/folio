# Valuation lenses — when the obvious multiple lies

> A 400-times earnings multiple is an easy no. Which is exactly why it deserves a second look.

> [!key] The issue's case studies share one lesson: **match the multiple to the business**. Healthcare Global (HCG), India's largest cancer-hospital chain, screens at a **~400× P/E** — but a decade of heavy capex (**~₹1,700 crore**: beds, LINACs, new cities, the Milann acquisition) loads depreciation onto the income statement, crushing EPS while EBITDA tells a steadier story. Bank of Baroda trades at **0.9× book** "like a batsman whose poor form has a long afterlife" — even with **RoA above 1%** and **RoE in the mid-teens** in FY26.

## The general rule

- **P/E** breaks on depreciation-heavy expansion, cyclical troughs, and turnarounds — earnings are the most manipulated and most temporarily-depressed line.
- **EV/EBITDA** sees through depreciation but forgives capital intensity — pair it with **ROCE** to catch capital burning (HCG's 12% is the honest number in that story).
- **Price-to-book + RoE/RoA** is the banking lens — and the "afterlife of poor form" is where its bargains hide.

> [!warn] Every multiple is a shortcut over assumptions. The screen's job is to *raise* the question; the case study's job is to answer it. HCG is simultaneously "not a 400× business" *and* "a 12%-ROCE business" — both lenses matter, and neither alone decides.

## Investor move

- Pick the **multiple that matches the business model**, not the default.
- Behind every optically absurd multiple, look for the **accounting cause** first.
- For banks: **book value + returns on it**; for capex stories: **EV/EBITDA + ROCE**, together.
