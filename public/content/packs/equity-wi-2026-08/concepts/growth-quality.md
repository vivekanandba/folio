# The kind of growth — Nvidia vs Coca-Cola

> Faster growth is only half the argument. The other half: what kind, how durable, and how much is already in the price.

> [!key] Samir Arora's paradox from the ET Alpha Wealth Summit: **Nvidia trades at a lower P/E than Coca-Cola** while growing earnings **60–70%** to Coke's **6–7%** — "ideally, that should not happen," yet **Coca-Cola outperformed Nvidia this year**. His blunt observation: *this is not how markets work* — the three questions that matter are **what kind of growth**, **how durable it is**, and **how much the market has already priced in**.

## The three questions, applied

- **Kind**: cyclical windfall, capex-fueled surge, or franchise expansion? Each kind decays differently.
- **Durability**: hot sectors attract capital and competition — the growth *rate* mean-reverts even when the company doesn't fail.
- **Priced in**: a P/E is a forecast. The "cheap fast grower" may be the market's way of saying it doubts year three.

> [!warn] The screen that flags "high growth + low P/E" as a bargain is often collecting exactly the businesses whose growth the market has correctly diagnosed as perishable. The anomaly is the message.

> [!more] The compounder connection
> This is the July issue's [wealth-creators lesson](#/pack/equity-wi-2026-07/concept/wealth-creators) at the buying end: wealth comes from growth that *persists* — rate × years — and the market pays most for the years, not the rate.

## Investor move

- Interrogate growth on **kind, durability, priced-in** — never rate alone.
- Treat a too-cheap fast grower as a **question, not a gift**.
- Pay for **predictability knowingly**, or bet against it knowingly — but know which you're doing.
