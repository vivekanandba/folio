# Investor wisdom — survivors, bottoms, and the other half of the plan

> The table records the winners. The many small companies that simply failed are not in it.

> [!key] Three columns worth keeping: the **survivorship warning plus the four-bottoms study** (from 2009/2012/2013/2016 bottoms, roughly **one listed company in ten** delivered outlier returns — hurdles of 31–38%/yr; from **2020, one in three** cleared a 47%/yr bar, because the *entry discount*, not skill, packed years of return into quarters); the **decumulation gap** (Suraj Kaeley's *The Desi Retirement Plan*: India teaches accumulating, not living off the corpus — "will your money outlive you, or will you outlive your money?"); and the **rupee's working lifetime** (₹9 per dollar in the early 1980s → ~₹96 today: more than 90% of its dollar value gone).

> [!warn] Both halves matter: the winners table is **survivorship-biased** (the failures aren't in it — the apparent ease is illusory), *and* market bottoms reliably mint a crop of outliers. Corrections are opportunity **because** most buyers won't act at them — not because everything bought there wins.

## The decumulation corrective

The SIP is "almost a civic virtue"; what's missing is the other half. Kaeley's frame: retirement is **a life stage, not a numerical milestone**, and money must be split by **job** — different pools for different tasks (healthcare gets special attention: medical costs are the retirement wildcard). One pool of money cannot do every job at once — the same conclusion [September's corrections](#/pack/finance-mfi-2026-09/concept/retirement-math) reached by arithmetic.

> [!more] The rupee question nobody asks
> ₹9 → ₹96 over a working lifetime is ~5.5% a year of quiet depreciation — the invisible tax on rupee-only wealth, and the standing argument for some hard-currency-linked earning power (exporters, global funds) inside an Indian portfolio. Most investors have "never spent a single evening asking why."

## Investor move

- Read every winners table with the **failures added back mentally**.
- Treat genuine market bottoms as **the sale they are** — pre-decide what you'd buy.
- Plan **decumulation by job**, and price the rupee's slow leak into long-term goals.
