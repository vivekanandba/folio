# Market discipline — statistics that want you to panic

> The Sensex fell 9.7% in H1 2026 — the worst first half since Covid. A statistic like that has one job.

> [!key] The issue's discipline thread: the **panic statistic** ("worst since 2020!") wants you to stop the SIP and wait — the historically losing move; the **daily P&L number** at the top of your portfolio screen "is there to hold your attention rather than inform you"; and a **doubling gold rally** quietly rebalanced portfolios in the wrong direction — the 5–10% bought as insurance became 15–20%, *"a bet that nobody actually decided to make."*

## Two reframes worth keeping

- **On robots and saving** (the Musk retort): if machines do the work, wages shrink — and **owning productive capital becomes the only claim on what the robots make**. A SIP is exactly that claim, bought monthly.
- **On recency bias**: the bearish client countering every datapoint with other people's gloom — while India contributes ~17% of global real GDP growth yet sits at roughly **half China's weight in MSCI EM**. Mean reversion is a patient argument.

> [!warn] Panic statistics are constructed backwards: "worst H1 since 2020" *selects* the window that maximizes alarm. The Covid half it cites was followed by one of the strongest recoveries on record — the statistic's own history refutes its advice.

> [!more] The attention diet
> Twenty-five years of the same advice: look at your portfolio **less often**. The daily number is engineered salience — it measures nothing your plan needs. The [four-pillar structure](#/pack/finance-mfi-2026-07/concept/four-pillars-planning) plus an annual rebalance beats a daily glance every time.

## Investor move

- A scary statistic is a **framing**, not a signal — check what window it chose.
- Rebalance by rule: **trim the doubled winner**, cut or redirect its SIP.
- Look less often; **own the claim on productive capital** and let it compound.
